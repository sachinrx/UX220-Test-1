Tim Berners-Lee and the World Wide Web

The World Wide Web Original Purpose

Tim Berners-Lee developed the World Wide Web at CERN, a particle physics research laboratory. Scientists needed a way to share research documents and access information across different computers and institutions.

The Web Today

In todays world, people use the Web for communication, education, entertainment, shopping, and business, as well as scientific research. Its audience now extends far beyond physics researchers.

Comparison

The original Web focused on sharing scientific information, while the Web today supports many parts of everyday life. Both of the uses rely on linking information and making it more easy for people to access and share.
